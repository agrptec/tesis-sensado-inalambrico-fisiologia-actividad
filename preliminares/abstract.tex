\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This dissertation investigates when wireless channels support the recovery of human activity and physiological patterns using Wi-Fi and LoRa infrastructure. A measurement does not directly observe the human state: it combines geometry, multipath propagation, waveform, antennas, hardware, and protocol. A physically structured wireless digital twin is proposed to model this chain, distinguish static from differential fidelity, and quantify resolvability, observability, and uncertainty before solving the inverse problem.

The current evidence uses DICHASUS dc01 for calibration and phase analysis. B0--B2 show that improving power, magnitude, and delay spread does not necessarily improve complex phase agreement. An effective delay component exhibits limited spatial transferability and improves the channel under stated conditions. S4.7-R shows that relative representations increase static spatial discriminability, mainly across regions, without establishing sensing or differential fidelity. S4.7-Ua finds no evidence of transferable circular predictability of the common phase from the evaluated descriptors; this is an operational limit rather than a claim of universal unpredictability. A pathwise audit verifies trajectory summation but reveals extreme numerical conditioning in one configuration: full algebraic rank does not imply robust coefficient identifiability. DICHASUS d010 provides complementary dynamic evidence, although transmitter motion is not equivalent to human motion with fixed terminals.

These findings redirect validation toward controlled physical perturbations. E0 will measure the instrumental floor and reference stability; E1 will impose known displacements to compare $\Delta H$ and measured versus simulated Jacobians; E2 will use a respiratory phantom before human experiments. Wi-Fi and LoRa are treated as different observation operators of the same physical state. The intended contribution is to establish which representation preserves sensitivity, when the twin reproduces that sensitivity, and when the available evidence requires uncertainty or abstention.
